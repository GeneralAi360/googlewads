# Creative disruption library

## Evidence status

This is an optional **creative-hypothesis library** adapted from the user-supplied presentation «ВИЗУАЛ И УПАКОВКА». It is not proof that unusual packaging, absurdity, emotion, paradox, or pain visualization increases CTR/CVR.

Use it only when the business problem benefits from a less literal presentation. Enterprise, healthcare, finance, legal, luxury, or other trust-sensitive categories may deliberately choose low disruption.

## Disruption level

Every idea architecture declares one:
- `LOW` — credibility, clarity, product truth, category maturity dominate;
- `MEDIUM` — one unusual device without sacrificing comprehension;
- `HIGH` — attention-first concept where surprise is part of the idea.

Higher is not better.

## Optional creative devices

### Contrast of meanings
Place two understandable meanings in tension.

Use when the contrast clarifies the proposition rather than creating random surrealism.

### Pain visualization
Make the user's problem tangible or visible.

Avoid fear/exaggeration that exceeds the verified claim.

### Outcome aesthetics
Show what the desired result feels or looks like rather than depicting a feature list.

### Visual paradox
Use an unexpected combination that remains quickly interpretable.

### Pseudo-reality
Present a fictional-but-obviously-conceptual object or situation to explain the idea.

Do not use pseudo-reality for product/UI proof that must be truthful.

### Personification / character
Turn an abstract problem/product behavior into a character or mascot.

Use only when the brand/emotional target supports playfulness or narrative.

### Genre mask
Express a familiar problem through a recognizable visual genre: editorial, diagram, comic, dossier, museum label, blueprint, etc.

### Unexpected comparison
Explain a benefit through a surprising but defensible analogy.

### Narrative packaging
Show a compact sequence: before/after, cause/effect, workflow, escalation, reveal.

### Absurd specificity
Use one exaggeratedly specific visual detail to make a generic idea concrete.

Keep unsupported literal claims out of the copy.

## Selection rule

A creative device must answer:
1. What business/communication problem does it solve?
2. What single takeaway does it improve?
3. Does it match the emotional target and visual character?
4. Can it remain legible in the target format?
5. Does lighting support the device or merely decorate it?
6. Does it conflict with brand, category trust, asset truth, or commercial locks?

If the answer is weak, use a more literal presentation mode.
